# Fashion E-commerce Browse/Catalogue UX: Competitive Research

**Purpose:** Find strong browse and catalogue patterns for an original clothing-store demo. We study how these sites work and do not copy any brand's look, copy or assets.
**Date:** 2026-11-02
**Sites:** Zara, COS, Arket, Uniqlo, H&M, Mango, Massimo Dutti, Everlane, Aritzia, & Other Stories

## Method and confidence (read this first)

- **Sources:** Web search across UX critiques, Baymard Institute pages, Econsultancy, UX Planet, UXmatters, Pratt IxD, Reddit/Substack commentary and vendor guidance.
- **Pages could not be opened.** The session's network allowlist blocked direct fetching of every page tried (baymard.com, uxmatters.com, ixd.prattsi.org, econsultancy.com all failed DNS). Sourced claims below therefore rely on **search-result excerpts**, not full-page reads. The brands' own sites were not opened live either.
- **How claims are tagged:**
  - **[S]** = backed by a cited source.
  - **[O]** = an observation from general familiarity with these sites. These describe typical layouts as of 2024–2025 and may have changed. Verify them on the live site before relying on them.
- **Baymard paywall:** Most of Baymard's per-site scores (H&M, Everlane and others) are behind its Premium plan. Only the headline verdicts that appeared in search snippets are used here.
- **Scores:** Each 1–5 score is my judgement from the evidence plus the [O] observations. They are not measured data.

## 1. Zara

| Area | Notes |
|---|---|
| Home page | Full-bleed editorial imagery or video, split by gender/department tabs. Baymard-assisted audit notes the homepage is "built entirely on carousels" [S: spyro-soft audit]. |
| Navigation | Hamburger drawer even on desktop, with tiny uppercase type. Critics say the nav text is too small and the hierarchy is off: an oversized logo next to tiny navigation text [S: UX Planet]. One Pratt critique was more positive about the hamburger as a section indicator [S: Pratt IxD]. |
| Category grid | Very dense. A zoom/columns toggle switches between roughly 2, 4 and 6+ columns, and Zara is credited with a button that expands the grid to full width [S: UXmatters]. Tall images of about 2:3 or 3:4. Infinite scroll, which critics say makes "New In" an "infinitely long page" [S: Pratt IxD]. |
| Filters & sort | Filters open in an overlay panel. Students and auditors call the filtering system inefficient [S: tingyu-fang case study]. Search is hard to find and returns irrelevant results [S: Zion & Zion white paper]. |
| Quick-add / size | A "+" on the card opens sizes. UXmatters says the "+" "appears more like an Add to Cart" and is unclear [S: UXmatters]. |
| Product card | Minimal: name and price in small type. Swatches appear inconsistently. Similar listings are hard to tell apart [S: Pratt IxD]. |
| Criticisms | Text unreadable without zoom, and a reviewer had to zoom to 50% to see a whole dress [S: UX Planet]. Rated average to poor on usability versus H&M in user research [S: uxdesign bootcamp case study]. "Appearance over usability" [S: UXmatters]. |

**Scores:** Clarity 2 · Speed to purchase 3 · Trust 3 · Mobile 3 · Accessibility 1

| Area | Notes |
|---|---|
| Home page | [O] Promotional and dense: price-led banners, "limited offer" modules and many category shortcuts. |
| Navigation | The 2024 desktop redesign repeats the navigation items in a left column, which pushes the filters off-screen [S: The Cognitive Load substack]. The app uses tabs instead of a hamburger menu [S: uxdesign.cc]. |

## Cross-cutting research findings (apparel)

- Most e-commerce filtering is rated "mediocre" to "poor" [S: Baymard filter UI].
- About 20% of sites fail to keep applied filters visible [S: Baymard filter UI].
- Filter state should survive a visit to a product page and back [S: Baymard filter UI].
- Keep the filter trigger visible while scrolling [S: Baymard filter UI].
- Show a result count for each filter value, and disable values that would return zero results [S: Shopify filtering UX].
- Users expect a filter for every key attribute shown in the listing [S: Baymard filter UI].
- Over 80% of apparel sites give insufficient sizing info [S: Baymard apparel research launch].
- Put the size filter at the top and expand it by default. Reportedly 39% of users find, after browsing, that items aren't available in their size [S: Tweakwise citing Baymard; not verified against Baymard directly].
- Size filters need clear grouping. Mixing alpha sizes with waist sizes caused mis-selection on Uniqlo [S: Baymard size-filter article].
- Use 2 columns below 768px [S: Depict, a vendor recommendation].

## Top 6 patterns to adopt

1. **Sticky filter and sort bar with applied-filter chips.**
   - Use a bottom sheet on mobile and a slide-in panel or left rail on desktop.
   - Keep the trigger visible on scroll and show active filters as removable chips above the grid.
   - Show a count on each value and persist state across product-page visits. (Baymard, Shopify)
2. **Size is a first-class facet.**
   - Put size first and expand it by default.
   - Group size systems clearly (Alpha, Waist, Numeric) and offer an "only show my size in stock" toggle. (Baymard, the Uniqlo test finding)
3. **Information-complete product card.**
   - Image at 3:4, a second image on hover or swipe, name, price, sale price with the original struck through, and up to about 5 colour swatches plus a "+N" count.
   - A single meaningful badge (New, Low stock), and optionally a rating and review count. (H&M swatches, Uniqlo ratings)
4. **Grid density toggle.**
   - Offer 1 or 2 columns on mobile and 3 or 4 on desktop, and remember the choice. This is an idea from Zara and Mango, but with readable type.
5. **Quick-add size row on desktop hover and a tap sheet on mobile.**
   - Use explicit size buttons labelled "Add size", never an ambiguous "+".
   - Grey out sold-out sizes and confirm with a mini-bag toast. (This avoids the UXmatters critique of Zara.)
6. **Trust and fit signals in browse.**
   - Show the free returns or shipping threshold in a slim header strip.
   - Show fit notes ("Runs small") and reviewer body data on product pages.
   - Add a size-recommendation helper. Transparent material or cost info is optional. (Uniqlo MySize, reviewer stats, Everlane transparency, which should be used honestly)

## 3 anti-patterns to avoid

1. **Style over legibility (Zara).** Avoid tiny uppercase nav and body text, a hamburger hiding all categories on desktop, and type that needs zooming. Use 14px or more for body and card text, meet WCAG AA contrast, and show visible top-level categories on desktop.
2. **Ambiguous icons and hidden core actions (Zara's "+", H&M's size-guide icon).** Every action needs a text label, and core product info should not be buried in tabs (Baymard on Everlane).
3. **Endless, stateless browsing.** Avoid infinite scroll with no position memory, carousel-only homepages, and filters pushed off-screen by repeated navigation (Zara, Uniqlo redesign). Use "Load more" with a visible "Showing 24 of 312" count, restore scroll position on Back, and keep filters above the fold.
